3D ropes: dragging stone and logs

What it is

Teams of workers pull ropes to drag a stone block on a wooden sled up a construction ramp, and they drag a felled log. Each rope is a chain of particles joined by distance constraints, solved position-based with substeps. One end of each rope is pinned to the sled, and the rope's pull goes back into the block as force and torque. The block slides against a terrain signed distance field (SDF) under Coulomb friction: it stays put until the pull beats μ·N.

Wetting the sand in front of the sled (shown in an Egyptian tomb painting; measured by Fall et al. 2014, Phys. Rev. Lett.) roughly halves the pull needed.

Controls

Left-drag: orbit. Wheel: zoom. Right- or shift-drag: pan. Left-drag a rope particle to tug it by hand.

Good at

  • Ropes stay stiff without exploding: the substeps keep each correction tiny.
  • Static vs sliding comes straight from Coulomb: nothing moves until the pull exceeds μ·N.
  • Deterministic: fixed step, fixed order, no randomness.

Bad at

  • The cost grows with particles × substeps × iterations.
  • A heavy worker on a light rope converges slowly (the mass-ratio problem); long-range constraints patch it.
  • The block is kept on the ground: it slides and turns but never tips or lifts.

In 3D / for our game:

Each block or log carries its own shape (its own field) and transform and asks the terrain SDF where the ground is. Ropes need a deterministic constraint solver. The number of ropes and workers is the CPU budget question: here it is particles × 20 substeps × 4 iterations, per team, every frame.
