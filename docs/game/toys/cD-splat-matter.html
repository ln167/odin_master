D — splats as physical matter

What it is

The scene is drawn as a few thousand soft coloured ellipses ("Gaussian splats"), and those same splat centres are the particles of a Material Point Method simulation (particles carry mass; a temporary grid works out forces). Each particle tracks how its neighbourhood has been stretched and sheared (the deformation gradient F), and the splat's shape is bent by the same amount, Σ' = FΣFᵀ. So one dataset is both the picture and the physics, with no mesh in between.

Controls

Drag across the sand to plough through it.

Live stats

Good at

  • One dataset for rendering and physics; nothing to keep in sync.
  • No meshing or remeshing, even when the mound splits apart.
  • Granular slumping, ploughing and splashing come out naturally.

Bad at

  • The interior must be filled with splats, or the hollow shell collapses.
  • Cost and memory scale with splat count, including hidden interior ones.
  • Big deformation stretches splats into streaky artefacts (clamped here).

In 3D / for our game:

Rubble piles and sand could be MPM splats directly, but a quarry face would need millions of filled interior splats; best reserved for the small region that is actually moving.
