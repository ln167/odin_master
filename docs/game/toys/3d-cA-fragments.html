3D: fragments carry their own field

What it is

The terrain is one formula (a signed distance field: distance to the surface, negative inside). Every block, log or statue carries its own small 24³ grid of signed distances in its own body frame, plus a transform. Collision is only "ask the other field how far this surface point is". Carving edits only that object's grid; splitting gives each half its own new grid and transform.

Controls

Left-drag rotate, wheel zoom, right-drag or shift-drag pan. Click an object to use the tool.

Inspect

Hover an object.

Stats

Good at

  • Promotion to rigid body = add a transform; the grid never moves, only the transform does.
  • Carving and splitting are local: only one small grid changes.
  • Collision and picking are plain field queries.

Bad at

  • Every object pays for its whole grid (55 KB here), even a thin sliver.
  • Fixed resolution per object: a big log gets coarse cells.
  • Contact by point sampling is approximate; stacks need sleeping to stay still.

In 3D / for our game: every quarried block, felled log or fragment is a field + transform; when it settles it can be welded back into the world field (min into the terrain) and its grid freed (not implemented here).
