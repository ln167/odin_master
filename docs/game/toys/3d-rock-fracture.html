3D - Rock fracture (bonded cell complex)

What it is

The rock is a complex of irregular convex cells (anisotropic Voronoi, squashed along bedding, cut by joint/bed planes) glued by bonds on their shared faces. The material sets cell shape and the bond law. A break is a min-cut over the bond graph, so cracks run along cell faces and every piece has sharp planar facets. The hammer refines cells on demand by splitting them with a few extra planes (flakes); plug-and-feather splits cells along the drilled plane so the plane exists in the complex.

Controls

Left-drag rotate, wheel zoom, right/shift-drag pan, click = use tool. Plug-and-feather: drag a line across the rock top, then Drive wedges.

Live stats

Good at

  • Every piece is a union of convex cells: flat facets, sharp edges, never round or voxel-stepped.
  • Material is data: cell anisotropy + bond law give granite chunks, sandstone beds, slate plates from the same code.
  • Detail only where it happens: the hammer splits 1-3 cells with flake planes; the drill splits only cells on its plane.
  • Fracture faces are known exactly (bond faces), so they get their own fresh-rock shading.

Bad at

  • Global max-flow per hit; the game needs a local window around the impact.
  • Rigid fragments only collide with the ground and the rock cells (vertex vs cell), not with each other.
  • Cell scale caps crack detail: a crack can't be finer than the cells unless they are refined first.
  • No stress field: the sledge uses damage falloff + min-cut, not real elastic waves.

In 3D / for our game:

Untouched cells are regenerated from a position hash on demand (seeds per grid cell, same clip code), so intact rock costs 0 bytes; only broken bonds, removed cells and refined pieces are stored (see "game delta" in stats). Fragments carry their cells + a transform.
