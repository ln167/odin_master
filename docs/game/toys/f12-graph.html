Load graph

What it is

A graph is just dots (nodes = chunks of rock with a mass) joined by lines (edges = contacts with a strength); it stores who touches whom and nothing about shape. Click a node to quarry it: anything no longer connected to the ground falls, then weight is pushed down the edges and overloaded edges snap.

Live stats

Good at

  • Tiny and exact: connectivity answers are yes/no, never fuzzy.
  • "What is holding what" is a cheap flood fill at coarse scale.
  • Thousands of nodes cost well under a frame.

Bad at

  • No shape at all: nothing says where a chunk is or how it breaks.
  • Cost grows with node count; millions of nodes get expensive.
  • Needs another representation for geometry.
  • Remove the arch keystone: only the capstone falls. A real arch collapses, because it stands on sideways thrust and balance, which "connected to ground + push weight down" cannot see. Arches need a balance/thrust check on top.

In 3D / for our game:

Use it as the coarse level: one node per big block or arch stone, edges for contacts, not per voxel.
