P2G / G2P — Material Point Method

What it is

MPM simulates matter as many particles (points that carry mass, velocity and deformation) plus a background grid (a fixed lattice of nodes) that is wiped and rebuilt every step. Each step it spreads particle data onto the grid (P2G, "particle to grid"), solves forces on the grid, then reads the new velocities back (G2P, "grid to particle"). This is the 2D MLS-MPM scheme from the 88-line "mpm88" example, written from scratch.

Takeaway: particles = the matter (identity, history); grid = scratch space rebuilt every step; P2G/G2P = the promotion maths, a template for every conversion we need.

Controls

Press "Phase step" to walk through one MPM step. Click the canvas to select a particle (yellow).

Live stats

Good at

  • Granular matter: crumbs, mud, sand and water in one method.
  • Mixing materials and topology change (splitting, merging) for free.
  • Particles keep history (how deformed, how compacted).

Bad at

  • Cost: every particle does P2G + G2P every substep.
  • Fine detail needs many particles (~4 per grid cell).
  • Tuning: stiffness forces tiny timesteps or it explodes.

In 3D / for our game: stencil becomes 3×3×3 = 27 nodes; use MPM only for active rubble/mud near the player, promote settled matter back to voxels.
