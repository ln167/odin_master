E - adaptive structured lattice (2D)

What it is

A cliff is a regular grid of right triangles that is coarse everywhere except where something hits it. Impacts split nearby triangles in half (bisection) until they are tiny, and neighbours are split too so no vertex sits in the middle of another triangle's edge ("conforming"). Bonds are triangle edges; an edge snaps when the damage on both sides beats the rock's strength, and chunks no longer attached to the cliff edge fall.

Controls

Click the rock to impact there.

Live stats

bytes = triangles x (12 idx + 1 mat + 4 dmg + 4 tree) = triangles x 21
uniform = 320 x 2^finest level triangles x 21

Good at

  • Detail only at the event; the rest stays at 320 triangles.
  • Fracture surfaces fall naturally on element faces.
  • Regular structure: a triangle is 3 indices plus a tree link.

Bad at

  • Conforming refinement (neighbour propagation) is fiddly.
  • Cracks still follow triangle edges, so they look jagged.
  • Coarsening must refuse to merge anything cracked or damaged.

In 3D / for our game:

BCC tetrahedra refined around each event, coarse across the mountain; regions stay cheap until struck.

Prior art:

Molino/Bridson/Fedkiw 2003 (BCC + red-green refinement), Koschier 2014 (adaptive tets for fracture), Pixelux DMM in The Force Unleashed (2008).
