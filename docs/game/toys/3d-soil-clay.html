3D soil: sand | loam | clay as bonded grains

What it is

Three plots, same tools, three materials. Soil is a few thousand small grains moved by position-based dynamics (substeps, a spatial hash for contacts, Coulomb friction at every contact; velocity = displacement / h). Neighbouring grains are joined by bonds (distance constraints). The bond law is the material, so the material decides how it breaks and where its weak points are.

Sand: no bonds, pours to its repose angle. Loam: brittle bonds, strong inside crumbs and weak between them, so it fails along crumb boundaries and falls as clods; weak bonds under tension creep and snap, so a cut face holds briefly, then crumbles. Clay: plastic bonds stretch and keep their new length up to a limit, and re-form when grains are pressed together, so it holds a vertical cut, keeps tool marks and sticks into lumps.

Controls

Camera: left-drag rotate, wheel zoom, right-drag or shift-drag pan. Ctrl+left uses the tool. "Cut trench walls" digs the front half of each plot out with spade bites and throws it on a spoil heap; watch the three walls. Hover a grain to inspect it.

Live stats

Good at

  • Material behaviour comes from one small law per bond: break threshold, plastic yield, re-form distance.
  • Fracture follows built-in weak points (loam crumb boundaries), not a grid.
  • Tool marks, clods and sticky lumps fall out of the same rule; nothing is scripted per tool.

Bad at

  • Cost scales with grains: every grain pays for contacts and bonds each substep, even at rest.
  • Grains are 2 cm: real sand is far finer, so each material is drawn as a surface over its grains (sand: one granular mass; loam: creased at crumb boundaries; clay: seams only between separate lumps) with sub-grain detail shaded on.
  • Position-based: stiffness and break strains depend on substep count; tune per step size.

In 3D / for our game: grains only where dirt is being worked (a trench face, a basket, a spoil tip). Settled dirt folds back into layered strata (cheap height layers with a material per layer) and is regenerated as grains on demand when a tool touches it (mentioned here, not implemented).
