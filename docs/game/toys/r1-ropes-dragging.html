r1 — Ropes, dragging, tree felling

What it is

A rope is a chain of particles joined by distance constraints, solved by position-based dynamics (PBD): move each particle freely, then repeatedly nudge pairs back to their rest distance, and read velocity as displacement / dt. One end is pinned to a point on a rigid body (the rope pulls it, and it pulls the rope back); the other end is held by a group of workers. The block slides on terrain given by a signed distance function, with Coulomb friction: it only moves when pull > μ·m·g.

Good at

  • Rope is cheap and unconditionally stable; two-way pull on rigid bodies.
  • Friction and the wet-sand trick (μ 0.6 → 0.3, Fall et al. 2014, Phys. Rev. Lett.) fall out of one rule.

Bad at

  • Stretch grows when a light rope hauls a heavy body (mass ratio); more iterations cost time.
  • Rope does not collide with the block or itself here.

In 3D / for our game:

Ropes + attachments need a deterministic rigid-body and constraint solver that hundreds of workers can pull on at 30 fps; blocks and trees are objects that each carry their own field and transform, sliding against the terrain field.
