F10 · Neural field

What it is

A small neural network (a stack of multiply-add-then-bend layers) is the world: you ask it f(x,y) and it answers "how much rock is here". We overfit it (train it until it memorises) to one hand-painted world, so its weights become a compressed copy of the grid. The coordinates are first turned into wiggles (Fourier features: sines at many frequencies) or looked up in small learnable grids (hash grid) so the network can draw sharp detail.

Live stats

Good at

  • High compression of one hand-crafted world (weights ≪ grid).
  • Continuous: sample at any resolution, between cells.
  • Smooth, plausible in-betweens instead of blocky cells.

Bad at

  • Edits: the network doesn't know until you retrain or add a patch on top.
  • Slow queries: thousands of multiply-adds vs one memory read.
  • Training time, and it never quite hits exact edges.

In 3D / for our game: bake the hand-built, untouched mountain into a network for storage/streaming; keep quarried and carved regions as a grid of residual patches on top.
